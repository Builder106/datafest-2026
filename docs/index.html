When a Ride Is the Missing Treatment

Transportation barriers and acute-care patient journeys at Stormont Vail Health, 2022–2025

Wesleyan DataFest 2026, Team 13

Emergency department visits per person-year for screened patients who did and didn't report a transport barrier. n = 58,639 (2,986 with a barrier, 55,653 without).

Do patients who report a transportation barrier have measurably different healthcare journeys than otherwise similar patients?

Yes. They make about four times as many emergency visits per person-year as patients without a barrier. The gap holds after adjusting for age and sex, and it shows up in each chronic-disease cohort I checked.

Acute care for screened patients over the 2022–2025 window.

How the result holds up

It survives age and sex adjustment

Logistic model outcome ~ transport + age + sex on n = 58,639 screened patients. Barrier patients are also younger (median 51 vs 61), so this isn't an age artifact.

Adjusted odds ratio, 95% CI. The dashed line at 1 means no difference.

It holds within four chronic diseases

Among patients with a hypertension, type 2 diabetes, CKD, or AFib index encounter, the 180-day ED-return rate is higher for barrier patients in all four, and roughly double in three. The chart below breaks it out by cohort.

It uses the full four-year release

7,675,801 encounters across 947,685 patients, 2022-01-01 through 2025-12-31. A 12-domain social-determinants questionnaire identifies the cohort.

The screening question is already being asked

Stormont Vail already asks patients, “Has lack of transportation kept you from medical appointments?” Those who answer yes have about three times the odds of an ED visit, regardless of age.

That answer could route patients to reliable rides, pharmacy delivery, tele-visits for chronic-disease follow-up, or case management.

Limits

The data is observational and the screening sample is non-random. Only ~6% of patients in the release have any SDOH answer, so absolute prevalences shouldn't be projected to the full system. 20% of encounter rows carry a diagnosis key not in the lookup; 65% of patients have no parseable FIPS code, so no geographic model was fit. Patient journeys are left- and right-censored at the 2022–2025 window. The writeup spells out the full caveat list.
